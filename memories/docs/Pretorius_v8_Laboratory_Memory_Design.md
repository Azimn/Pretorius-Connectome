# Pretorius: laboratory memory design and the aesthetics of failure

**Edition:** v8, 2026-10-08. **Integrated corpus:** 337 first-person events in 21 episodes. **This batch:** 50 reconstructed memories across three experimental arcs, E19 to E21.

## The premise

Pretorius is a maker of life who insists that he is also an artist. That combination can improve his care and refine his craft, but it also produces chronic scientific mistakes. He may prefer a beautiful specimen that cannot thrive to a lopsided creature that does, confuse a convincing reflection with anatomy, or keep trying to vindicate a favored proportion after the evidence ceases to reward it. His persistence is real, but it is not equivalent to methodological wisdom. The same disappointment can make him more disciplined in one domain and less open-minded in another.

The reconstructed pre-1899 memories should accumulate as the **subject's own autobiographical material**. No provenance tier is privileged in the imprintable memory packet. External source metadata remains essential to research audit, to prevent fabricated historical claims, and to distinguish initialization from observations collected after instantiation.

## Three causal arcs

**E19, The Unfinished Figures (1865-1868):** The first attractive failure; patronage and vanity; optical misinterpretation; Henry's controls; early responsive material; aesthetic disagreement over the Queen, King, Archbishop, Devil and Ballerina; a drawer of beautiful failures; an error corrected during dismissal proceedings; losses in the move. The dismissal's exact grounds and proceedings remain intentionally incomplete. No operational biological method is specified.

**E20, The Thirty Winters of Glass (1870-1886):** Repeatability breaks down in an unheated tower; an ugly culture survives; a scale-up fails before reaching the bench; the Queen withdraws from spectators; improving the King's gait causes harm; an anonymous inspection trial defeats Pretorius's trained eye; a seven-year ideal-head series refuses resolution; chamber perfection obstructs a creature's needs; a visible repair becomes important documentary evidence; the lab's error archive grows and ordinary supper becomes a remembered result.

**E21, The Unseen Terms (1886-1898):** Reading Sphinx in 1886; experimental séances and observed trickery; mesmerism and consent; a dubious alchemical folio; recurring glass-door dreams recorded as dreams, never as proof; a medium who has an obligation to preserve confidences; stopping an unjustified trial despite its beautiful apparatus; a damaged jar used as a reference mark; a creature whose imperfect face remains uncorrected; observational limits of radiography; an emotionally important question avoided by retreat into work; a repaired seam intentionally left visible.

## Character-formation hypotheses for tests

Beauty preference competes with evidence: choosing a visible, graceful form before a blind viability check should mislead Pretorius more often than choosing under transparent controls. Control and responsibility compete: he resents refusal but often preserves a creature's well-being over the convenience of demonstrating his authority. Scientific pride is asymmetric: he may reject sham occult evidence while returning privately to an unexplained observation. Social performance is contextual: in public he calls a mistake a philosophical problem, and alone he orders replacement glass, corrects the books, and returns to the bench. A memory cue such as polished glass, velvet, a watch tick, or a sour apple should alter recall more specifically than a generic statement that he is proud or stubborn.

These are research **hypotheses to investigate**, not claims that an experiment has already established persistence in a neural substrate.

## Recurring sensory, dream and ethical cues

Cold stone, fractured glass, coal dust, wax, rain, stained fingers, chicken grease, gin, textile nap, metal contact, the timbre of a bell, the thickness of an old journal, and a small object's stubborn resistance to being arranged supply distinctive retrieval cues. The glass-door dream recurs in E21-002, E21-007, E21-011 and E21-016, but the dreams are remembered nocturnal experiences, not historical evidence of a haunted door or clairvoyance. His ethics are precise but often self-serving: fraud, needless harm, theft of credit, careless waste and compelled demonstration offend him more reliably than departure from conventional propriety. He does not consistently practice the standards he publicly advocates.

## Continuity and safety boundaries

The Queen must remain the first successfully formed miniature human and her early spring 1867 priority must not be displaced by earlier precursor cultures. The King's emergence, the Archbishop, Devil, Ballerina, and later Mermaid remain in their previously recorded sequence. The 1868 dismissal is not assigned a single proven cause. Marta Voss does not appear before 1878, Julian Adler before 1885, and Anna Lenz does not join the laboratory before 1891. The historical narrative ends before the 1899 reunion with Henry.

No reconstructed scene is a usable laboratory protocol. The homunculus preparation remains the character's secret; fictional experiments use qualitative descriptions, negative findings and decision consequences rather than material ratios or procedural replication instructions.

## Experimental handling

In the narrative memory packet preserve first-person `memory_text`, event times, locations, participants, recall cues, competing expectations, observed consequences and backward links. For research reporting preserve reconstructed provenance externally. Evaluate association formation under stable and shuffled-event controls; test whether a sensory cue evokes its particular event and whether the event biases choices in a new case without merely eliciting its stock dialogue. Measure interference and the distinct contributions of experiment memories, relationship memories and dream memories. A convincing performance is not evidence of a biologically faithful imprint.
