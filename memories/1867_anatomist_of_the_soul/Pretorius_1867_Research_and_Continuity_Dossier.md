# Research and continuity dossier: Pretorius's 1867 soul memoir

**Status:** External editorial and historical research metadata. **Fictional paper:** [On the Supposed Seat of the Soul, and the Persistence of Nervous Excitability](Pretorius_1867_On_the_Seat_of_the_Soul.md). **Linked newspaper artifact:** [Alcide Jolivet's French article](Pretorius_Jolivet_1867_French_Original.txt). **Memory integration:** not yet performed.

## Scope and provenance

The journal paper is a **complete fictional scientific memoir** written in period-informed English, as though translated from a hypothetical 1867 German manuscript. Its author, clinical or experimental series, numbers, results, date, acknowledgments, and placement in the periodical are invented components of the Pretorius literary continuity. This document must never be represented as a newly found nineteenth-century scientific article, and its observations must not be cited as genuine historical evidence. The experimental figures are invented to be plausible and modest, not verified or reproducible empirical data.

The cited historical scientific books and journal articles are real. The invented institutional setting is an alternate-history Ingolstadt university, which remains operative in the character's pre-1868 biography even though the historical institution moved. This divergence is deliberate and should not be normalized away.

## Intended period document

- **Literary author:** Septimus Pretorius, attached to the philosophy faculty in Ingolstadt, age approximately twenty-five.
- **Manuscript date:** 18 April 1867, invented.
- **Fictional publication:** May 1867 circulation, a timing chosen for the story.
- **Putative venue:** *Zeitschrift für rationelle Medicin*, Third Series, volume 29 (a real volume issued in 1867). The fictional article was never published in that volume. There are no invented page numbers or DOI.
- **Original language in-world:** German, presumed. The extant manuscript is an original English reconstruction for the project, not a translation of a German source.
- **Title in the manuscript:** *On the Supposed Seat of the Soul, and the Persistence of Nervous Excitability*.
- **Working German counterpart, not a historical bibliographic entry:** *Ueber den vermeintlichen Sitz der Seele und die Fortdauer der nervösen Reizbarkeit*.

## Historical foundations, checked to precede the story date

**The journal.** *Zeitschrift für rationelle Medicin* existed and published a third-series volume 29 in 1867. The Wellcome catalogue gives its third-series run through 1869. The actual contents of the 1867 journal are not being altered or represented as though they contain Pretorius's paper.

- Wellcome Collection: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/kgpzs55y
- Archival catalogue entry for third-series volume 29 (1867): https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Zeitschrift_f%C3%BCr_rationelle_Medicin._Volume_29_(Third_Series),_1867._(IA_s2495id1379973).pdf

**Experimental irritability and animal electricity.** Du Bois-Reymond's original investigations into animal electricity were published beginning in 1848 and continued in 1849. Nerve, muscle, electrical responses, and comparatively sensitive instruments were all legitimate subjects in 1867. The invented Pretorius paper does not assume later neural signalling theory or electrophysiological instruments.

- ETH Zürich catalogue: https://www.e-rara.ch/zut/content/titleinfo/16865143
- Historical analysis of equipment and methods: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4588001/

**Conduction and timing.** Helmholtz had already published a preliminary report on the propagation velocity of nervous excitation in 1850. A light lever, smoked recording surface, induction apparatus, and timing of a contraction are therefore period plausible. The fictional numbers in Pretorius's paper are not ascribed to Helmholtz.

- Historical report: https://wellcomecollection.org/works/nm26r2xz
- Recovered 1850 bibliographical listing: https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Hermann_von_Helmholtz

**Spinal reflexes.** Eduard Pflüger's *Die sensorischen Functionen des Rückenmarks der Wirbelthiere* (1853) provides a real historical starting point for complicated reflex movements in preparations whose higher cerebral connections are absent. The memoir does not claim that reflex action proves the absence of sensation.

- Wellcome Collection: https://content.www.wellcomecollection.org/works/ehggr59p

**Psychophysics.** Fechner's *Elemente der Psychophysik* (1860) established an empirical programme for studying correspondences between physical stimulus and reported sensation. Pretorius invokes methodological distinction, rather than anachronistic twentieth-century models of the mind.

- Google Books bibliographic record: https://books.google.com/books?id=FDEFAQAAIAAJ

**Cerebral localisation and speech.** Broca's 1861 paper connected a case of loss of articulate speech with a cerebral lesion. His work does not warrant treating every aspect of personality as a neatly isolated organ or equating speech with all consciousness.

- Original French text and citation, *Bulletin de la Société anatomique*, 6, 330-357: https://psychclassics.yorku.ca/Broca/aphemie.htm
- Max Planck bibliography: https://www.mpi.nl/publications/item2301213/remarques-sur-le-siege-de-la-faculte-du-langage-articule-suivies-dune

**Experimental method.** Claude Bernard's *Introduction à l'étude de la médecine expérimentale* was published in 1865. The fictional article's distinction between observation, hypothesis, repeated test, and uncertainty reflects this contemporary methodological culture.

- Original text and bibliographic record: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Introduction_%C3%A0_l%E2%80%99%C3%A9tude_de_la_m%C3%A9decine_exp%C3%A9rimentale

**A contemporary philosophical counterpoint.** Paul Janet's *Le Cerveau et la Pensée* appeared in 1867, developing pieces first published in 1865. The newspaper article by Jolivet invokes Janet in August 1867; the Pretorius paper of April 1867 deliberately does not rely on the book's uncertain month of release.

- 1867 book: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Cerveau_et_la_Pens%C3%A9e_%281867%29
- Janet's foreword, identifying the original 1865 essays: https://fr.wikisource.org/wiki/Le_Cerveau_et_la_Pens%C3%A9e_%281867%29/Avant-propos

## Fictional experimental design and limitations

The scientific article reports three imaginary observational series:

**Series I: nerve-and-muscle persistence.** Twelve frog preparations are followed for up to approximately sixty minutes after separation. The table's descending numbers of recordable contractions are entirely fictional, intended to depict deteriorating local excitability rather than continued consciousness or the survival of a person. The observations do not establish a universal duration of post-separation responsiveness, and the paper explicitly acknowledges effects of repeated testing, temperature, and variable preparation conditions.

**Series II: spinal reflexes.** Nine fictional preparations show withdrawal, contralateral participation, and posture-dependent changes. These are consistent with the *kind of question* studied by nineteenth-century reflex physiologists; the reported individual outcomes are invented. The article consistently separates apparently adaptive movements from proof of subjective experience.

**Series III: previous impressions.** Eight intact frogs are observed under repeated harmless shadows. In five, the tendency to withdraw appears to diminish; changes in lighting revive movement in three. These figures are fictional and intentionally fail to settle whether fatigue, habituation, lighting, or posture explains the effect. The writing avoids claiming that an inward memory was measured. The separate secret experiments involving developing homunculi are not published here.

None of these series constitutes the isolation or detection of a soul. The paper's speculative turn, in section IX, considers whether creating certain forms of biological organisation might ultimately become a legitimate experimental question. It makes no claim to have succeeded.

The authoritative main corpus is currently **version 9, 385 first-person memories**, ending in June 1899 before Pretorius meets Henry Frankenstein again. The university era is protected for the pending new authorial arc. The journal and newspaper documents are **source artifacts**, not additions to the current memory JSONL.

Heller's insistence on distinguishing evidence and conjecture is compatible with a restrained printed memoir. Henry's involvement in theoretical disputes remains part of the private university story. None is assigned a newly established action in the existing records by this paper's publication.

The character's first coherent secret developing preparation is already placed in **February 1867**, and the Queen's emergence and naming follow in spring 1867. The paper therefore **must not** expose the existence, methods, names, physical appearance, or other confidential observations of the homunculi. It describes prior conventional physiological experiments; its speculative conclusion may reflect private ambitions that the author has not revealed.

Jolivet's separate fictional *Le Figaro* report is dated **28 August 1867**. It accurately describes a paper on the supposed seat of the soul, survival of local nervous excitability, reflexes, and the possibility that organized action is not identical with consciousness. The reporter notes that the paper is cautious, but detects in conversation a greater willingness to contemplate the artificial production of conscious life. The published paper's section IX gives the precise opening for that question while maintaining professional deniability.

The interview is not the sole reason Pretorius later leaves university. The 1868 dismissal remains a multifactorial institutional event involving concerns about laboratory secrecy, methodological responsibility, and the doctor's increasingly rigid relationships.

## Intended scientific-personality functions

The paper embodies real virtues as well as the seed of future excess: the ability to formulate falsifiable distinctions, unwillingness to infer consciousness from a muscle twitch, respect for repeated measurements, intolerance for sloppy reasoning, and a desire to be first to ask a question of great importance. At the same time, its final section advertises a theory of deliberate construction that the evidence cannot yet support. This is not fraud in the document. It is a revealing speculative ambition.

The later memory records should permit competing interpretations. Pretorius may remember the publication as rigorous, the journalism as opportunistic, the moral criticism as hostile, the academic response as mixed, and the accumulated scandal as partly of his own making. These judgments need not resolve into a single retrospective moral.

The period paper itself should not contain knowledge of events beyond April 1867, whether the Paris interview, the eventual 1868 expulsion, the 1899 pre-reunion stopping point, or the fictional world's future.
