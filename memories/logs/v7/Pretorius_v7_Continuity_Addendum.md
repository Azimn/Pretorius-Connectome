# Continuity Addendum v7: Intimacy, Social Risk and Work

All 27 v7 events are **reconstructed**, with new stable IDs E16-001 through E18-010. All 260 earlier autobiographical texts are retained unchanged. The fictional biography ends before direct reunion with Henry in 1899.

## People and relationships

**Theodor Vahl:** Adult draughtsman and stage designer met through Leonie Rausch in 1861. Technical friendship becomes reciprocal physical and romantic intimacy by 1862. The pair separate in 1866 when Vahl accepts outside work and Pretorius refuses to request he stay, after several missed commitments and evasive public choices. Their correspondence resumes in a different, respectful register by 1877; Vahl reports an adult male companion without this becoming a punishment or catastrophe. Items: borrowed optical lens, stage perspective drawing, ruler by window, scarf, two separate studio photographs and joint receipt.

**Max Reiss:** Adult schoolteacher, not a romantic partner in this edition. Consults Mathilde Rosen and Pretorius over a colleague's threatened reputation in 1873; exposes the limits of Pretorius's concept of safety in 1879; challenges clinical pathologizing and casual insult in 1881. Relocates abroad in 1884. Survives in correspondence and makes available evidence of a queer social world beyond the narrator's private dramas.

**Julian Adler:** Previously established in v6 as a Viennese instrument dealer and restorer. V7 confirms mutual romantic and bodily affection in 1888. Both are adults and neither is subordinate to the other. Agreement on exclusivity or household structure is never established. Pretorius values Julian's care and work, infringes third-party correspondence privacy in 1890, and learns only partially to respect distinct commitments. In 1898 Julian is living his own life and affectionate contact is unresolved, not extinguished. Julian **must not** be treated as dead, imprisoned or permanently resident at the tower on this evidence.

**Leonie Rausch, Clara Weiss, Mathilde Rosen, Marta Voss:** Retain their prior autonomous professional and personal histories. They respond to Pretorius's relationships with discretion, criticism, hospitality or technical demands rather than functioning as moral dispensers. No scene changes their pre-existing narrative memories.

## Distinct motivations

Scientific ambition is **not** invented as a compensatory response to queer persecution. It is already evident by university matriculation. In the new memories, vocational absorption is at times genuine, at times strategic avoidance, and frequently both. His love of men and reluctance to accept another person's claims are compatible, not synonymous. His desire for autonomy sometimes clashes with a partner's equivalent autonomy.

## Historical constraints

Pre-1871 Bavaria and post-1871 Germany cannot be collapsed into one legal regime. Vienna follows Austrian law; London follows UK law. Pretorius reads Ulrichs in 1867; this is **not** a fictional meeting. Wilde's conviction occurs 1895; Hirschfeld's Committee 1897. No characters speak using post-Victorian identity categories as if those were already universal.

## Unresolved threads

Pretorius never decisively settles whether he wants a shared domestic arrangement; Julian does not suspend his life awaiting that settlement. Theodor remains at a respectful distance. Reiss's later friendships and safety are his own story, not Pretorius's possession. Pretorius has not resolved the distinction between allowing autonomy and enjoying it. None of these threads should be falsely concluded in the pre-reunion period.
