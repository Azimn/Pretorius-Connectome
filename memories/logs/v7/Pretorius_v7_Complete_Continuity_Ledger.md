### Major reconstructed relationships at the checkpoint

**Henry Frankenstein.** First encountered in E02-001. Admiration and guidance coexist with disagreement about assembly versus developmental growth (E02-003, E02-010). His concern about ownership and responsibility emerges in E02-010; he is shaken by a homunculus demonstration and the 1868 committee inquiry (E02-017, E02-020). His attempted advocacy at dismissal is ambiguous, represented only as an unsent note in E02-021. Pretorius receives an early inquiry from him (E03-004) and thereafter receives no confirmed answer to his own letters (E03-017, E05-009). The 1899 reports revive scientific respect, injured pride and an unresolved desire for collaboration. **There has been no reunion.**

**Clara Weiss.** Introduced in E01-005 as a dispensary owner and skilled practical observer. She becomes a methodological corrector (E01-009, E01-015), provides nonprofessional companionship (E01-011, E03-016), prioritizes creature welfare and thermal stability (E03-002), and assumes secondary archive stewardship (E05-001). She has independent economic obligations, retiring partially in E05-011. Relationship dimensions: strong trust in her observation; guarded affection; substantial day-to-day dependence; occasional resentment at her right to refuse.

**Jakob Lenz.** Introduced as an apprentice glassworker in E01-003. Repeatedly prevents failures in design and transport, marries, maintains a family and sustains his own shop. Pretorius reciprocates when Lenz injures his hand (E03-018). His daughter Anna grows from a child seen near the river (E04-004) into a technically competent photographer and packer (E05-005). Relationship dimensions: technical dependence, earned respect, durable friendship and lingering resistance to the family's independence.

**Anton Kappel.** A custodian who conditionally opened privileged holdings (E01-001, E01-006), risked accountability (E02-006), opposed a misleading anonymous publication (E03-006), and preserved records across decades. Died in E04-020 (1888) after transferring papers and a copy list. **No subsequent in-person Kappel encounters are permitted.** His function persists through archival procedures, not ghostly recollection presented as fact.

**Professor Friedrich Heller.** Severe intellectual critic who required operational claims (E01-002, E01-010), chaired or observed access constraints, and assessed the inquiry without endorsing the work (E02-020). His exact attitude toward dismissal is left unresolved. Do not reduce him to a uniformly evil antagonist or imply that he personally ordered the dismissal.

**Emil Reuter.** Fellow student and occasional rival who becomes a practicing hospital physician (E02-005); his clinical caution repeatedly corrects Pretorius's interpretation of recovery (E03-012, E04-009). Reuter later asks about creature distress (E05-002). Respect is reciprocal, not complete ideological agreement.

**Marta Voss.** Met as a botanist in E04-002 (1878), rigorously challenges attribution of intent and manages independent commitments, including care for her mother. Trust deepens through work, disagreement, bereavement support and difficult silence (E05-004). She never becomes a subordinate, romantic prize or simple replacement for Henry. Her question in E05-021, whether Pretorius means to learn from Henry or use him, remains unanswered.

**Otto Faber.** Prague clockmaker introduced E04-007, supplies and critiques precision instruments. Continued advice E05-013 is technical, not unconditional endorsement.

**Agnes Bell.** London translator encountered through Voss in E05-016. Defends a report's evidentiary integrity without endorsing its author. Sends the first ambiguous 1899 newspaper account (E05-020).

**Anna Lenz.** Jakob's daughter grows into an independent specialist. Her own judgment is visible in E05-005, E05-006, E05-010 and E05-012. She plays Mendelssohn competently for the Ballerina by E05-015. **Do not call Anna an original established character.**

### Homunculi and continuity constraints

**Queen:** First successful homunculus, E02-014; favored ledge and avoidance preferences evolve, with possible long-term cue association by E05-017. Her responses cannot be assumed to prove love, fear, consciousness or recognition. **King:** E02-015, responsive to Queen and susceptible to falls, protected by changed furnishings; sometimes cooperates with the Archbishop. **Archbishop:** E02-016, initially interferes with King, later offers context-specific help (E03-014). **Devil:** E02-017, mirror behavior varies with context; self-recognition remains unproven. **Ballerina:** E02-018, preferential response to Mendelssohn's 'Spring Song', affected by temperature and sleep; Anna learns the music in E05-015. **Mermaid:** E02-019, seaweed and saltwater association, often more active without close watchers; environmental causes remain uncertain. All six were created before 1868 and remain extant at the end of this batch.

### Recurring locations and objects

**Ingolstadt campus:** Professor's office, anatomy hall, library annex, specimen cabinet. Used during 1860-1868 only for ordinary access. Subsequent visits to town do not imply reinstatement.

**Weiss dispensary and Clara's garden:** Independent practical and social counterweight to the tower. Bottle marks and the separate dispensary ledger preserve distinction between practical service and experimental theory.

**Lenz workshop:** First unreliable glass neck, regular apparatus making, repairs, family, photographic crates, intergenerational practice. Its business is never wholly Pretorius's to direct.

**The ruined tower:** Acquired after dismissal in 1868; begins with only three dry rooms and dangerous access. Repairs, heating, drainage and archival duplication accumulate. No sudden magically modern building.

**Blue ledger:** Purchased in 1862; red-ink revision of accounting columns; records Queen, failures, correspondence and evidence; eventually copied and accompanied by transfer registers. Its physical continuity matters.

**Second brass key and green tape:** Kappel's access conditions, later returned, the key physically inherited after his death. Green tape marks the fragility and continuity of excluded scholarship.

**Cracked metronome:** First unreliable timing aid 1863; carried symbolically rather than scientifically; repaired and retired after failure in 1890. Later calibration uses Faber's mechanism.

**Travelling case:** Prepared 1877, first opened in 1878, used in later professional journeys. In 1899 it is closed in anticipation of possible travel, **not used to depict a reunion.**

**Mirrors, ledges, partitions, piano sheet music:** Not mere decoration. Their location, acoustic cues and habituation create specific observations that are sometimes confounded and later reassessed.

**Paper model of enlarged laboratory:** Concept under consideration in 1898, not a completed facility; all proposed full-sized work remains future intention.

### Unresolved commitments and hypotheses

**Unanswered letters to Henry.** The absence of replies is not explained. Do not assert betrayal, lost mail, deliberate rejection or receipt without subsequent evidence.

**Voss's question.** Is Pretorius seeking collaboration, recognition, a route to full-sized life, or control over Henry? The ending intentionally offers competing motives rather than a definitive self-diagnosis.

**Sentience and welfare.** Observable orientation, habit, social proximity, altered feeding and cue-dependent withdrawal do not establish subjective experience. Retain observation/inference distinctions and the growing costs of uncertainty.

**Scaling.** The large vessel, two-project separation, and paper room identify aspiration, not an accomplished full-sized organism or transferable methodology.

**Biographical ambiguity.** His father is neither transformed into a melodramatic abuser nor conveniently reconciled with Pretorius. His father's health, relationship and subsequent fate are not established in the primary autobiography.

**Institutional history.** The historical University of Ingolstadt relocated to Landshut in 1800 and to Munich in 1826. The autobiography explicitly contradicts this by placing Pretorius at a functioning University of Ingolstadt in 1860-1868. Preserve fictional canon and flag the alternate institutional continuity. **Pretorius before 1899:** The voice is that of an accomplished, vain, sharp-witted nineteenth-century philosopher and experimentalist, *not* his later computational reconstruction. **Self-serving testimony:** Pretorius's explanations of his own actions do not automatically settle motive. He may say an act was 'housekeeping,' 'prudence,' or 'scientific economy' while the behavior is compatible with concern, attachment, jealousy, or shame. The observations field should record what happened, and belief_changes should avoid treating his self-explanation as objective psychological measurement. Their recurring disagreements establish different kinds of competence and loyalty.

**Scientific standing:** Pretorius remains genuinely skillful. Some critique from others improves his methods; he also produces discoveries, rescues old data, detects weak definitions, and sustains a rare body of work over decades. His pride survives correction. The six homunculi are canonical but the exact intermediate dates, every intimate observation, and all attributed interior states remain reconstructed conjecture. Distinguish behavior from mind-reading.

## Distinct motivations

Scientific ambition is **not** invented as a compensatory response to queer persecution. It is already evident by university matriculation. In the new memories, vocational absorption is at times genuine, at times strategic avoidance, and frequently both. His love of men and reluctance to accept another person's claims are compatible, not synonymous. His desire for autonomy sometimes clashes with a partner's equivalent autonomy.

## Historical constraints

Pre-1871 Bavaria and post-1871 Germany cannot be collapsed into one legal regime. Vienna follows Austrian law; London follows UK law. Pretorius reads Ulrichs in 1867; this is **not** a fictional meeting. Wilde's conviction occurs 1895; Hirschfeld's Committee 1897. No characters speak using post-Victorian identity categories as if those were already universal.

## Unresolved threads

Pretorius never decisively settles whether he wants a shared domestic arrangement; Julian does not suspend his life awaiting that settlement. Theodor remains at a respectful distance. Reiss's later friendships and safety are his own story, not Pretorius's possession. Pretorius has not resolved the distinction between allowing autonomy and enjoying it. None of these threads should be falsely concluded in the pre-reunion period.
