# Film and Historical Research: Queer Life in Pretorius's Reconstructed Past

**Status:** Contextual research and critical interpretations. None of the reconstructed romances or incidents below are claims that the original film explicitly supplies them.

## The film's queer legacy and interpretive disagreements

James Whale's 1935 *Bride of Frankenstein* is widely read as queer-coded, especially through Ernest Thesiger's stylized Pretorius, his relationship to Henry, and the collaboration of men in non-reproductive creation. The film does not identify Pretorius's sexuality. Later critics and historians have drawn distinct conclusions about the degree of intention and the proper interpretation of his mannerisms. Neither a camp performance nor a director's sexual identity establishes the fictional character's private biography. See Smithsonian Magazine's discussion of Laura Pheasant-Kelly and James Curtis; see also scholarly study of cuts and film censorship.

Sources: https://www.smithsonianmag.com/history/in-bride-of-frankenstein-the-monsters-wife-never-speaks-now-maggie-gyllenhaals-the-bride-gives-the-iconic-character-a-voice-180988257/ ; https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/01439685.2025.2593316

## Actual changes to legal and social conditions, 1860 to 1899

**Bavaria before 1871.** The Bavarian criminal code of 1813 left consensual same-sex conduct between adults outside the offense subsequently associated with Paragraph 175, although social exposure, employment risk, family pressure, and other penalties could still matter. It would be false to treat 1862 Ingolstadt as though the German Empire's 1871 statute were already in force. The later German Constitutional Court's historical discussion expressly notes the Bavarian distinction. https://www.servat.unibe.ch/dfr/bv006389.html

**1867 and 1871.** Karl Heinrich Ulrichs publicly defended same-sex-attracted men at a Munich jurists' congress in 1867, following pamphlets published from 1864. The 1871 German Imperial Penal Code introduced Paragraph 175 to Bavaria. The new scenes portray Pretorius as a reader of the pamphlets, **not** as an unverified personal acquaintance of Ulrichs. https://forummuenchen.org/projekte/steckbriefe-zu-lgbtiq-persoenlichkeiten/karl-heinrich-ulrichs/ ; https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Strafgesetzbuch_f%C3%BCr_das_Deutsche_Reich_%281871%29

**Vienna.** The Austrian code of 1852's section 129 penalized acts between people of the same sex, with a different statutory scope from the German law. Julian's Vienna home therefore cannot be treated as equivalent to Bavaria. https://de.wikisource.org/wiki/Strafgesetz_1852_%28%C3%96sterreich%29

**Britain and 1895.** The UK Criminal Law Amendment Act of 1885 criminalized gross indecency between men; Oscar Wilde's conviction in 1895 and the public circulation of private letters supply a plausible transnational shock. Wilde does not appear as Pretorius's personal acquaintance. https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-topic/identity-and-community/the-life-and-court-trials-of-oscar-wilde/

**1897.** Magnus Hirschfeld co-founded the Scientific-Humanitarian Committee. Pretorius may plausibly hear of its petitioning by 1897, without being a member or an associate of Hirschfeld. https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/magnus-hirschfeld-2

## Language and historical method

*Queer* is used by the editor as a retrospective umbrella. Pretorius does not retrospectively label his younger self with twenty-first-century identity language. The nineteenth-century terms used in his world were varied and sometimes contested. Ulrichs's *Urning* and sexological categories emerged in the 1860s, and different individuals understood their desires and alliances differently. Consult Douglas Pretsell, *Urning: Queer Identity in the German Nineteenth Century*, and Simon Joyce, *LGBT Victorians*, for the diversity of classification, community, intimate friendship, law and social presentation. https://www.jstor.org/stable/10.3138/jj.11589050 ; https://academic.oup.com/book/44044

## Fictional reconstruction choices

Theodor Vahl is an **invented adult peer and partner** in the 1860s. His relationship with Pretorius is explicitly romantic and physically affectionate, with eventual separation due to incompatible priorities and choices rather than a compulsory tragic punishment. Max Reiss is an **invented teacher and friend** who illustrates uneven vulnerability, mutual aid and independence of social identity from Pretorius's self-centered story. Julian Adler is an **already established fictional character in v6** whose affection is made explicitly reciprocal here beginning in 1888. Existing v6 scenes of professional regard, distance, privacy violations, and unsent letters remain textually unchanged and gain additional context. Neither relationship is offered as an exclusive explanation of Pretorius's devotion to science. Pretorius's attachment to his work predates these romances, and he uses it both productively and as a way to evade demands he cannot direct.

## Cautions for neural imprinting

These events are invented autobiographical accounts. First-person recollection is unreliable: a claim about an intention or another person's state is not a verified observation. Do not train the system to identify same-sex desire as a disorder, proof of villainy, or cause of emotional deficiency. No source demonstrates that the film character had these particular lovers. The new records provide testable cues, competing demands, boundaries and recurring relationships, not clinical diagnoses.
