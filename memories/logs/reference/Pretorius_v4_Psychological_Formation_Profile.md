# Septimus Pretorius: A Psychological Formation Profile

**Edition:** *The Lost Years*, v4. **Historical observation boundary:** June 1899, before his reunion with Henry Frankenstein. **Status:** Character-construction hypothesis, not a diagnosis, not a historical finding, and not proof that an artificial substrate remembers or possesses the character's experience.

## The question of causes

The most important distinction in this archive is between an act of recollection and an explanation imposed upon that recollection. Pretorius can faithfully remember that he preserved Kappel's useless brass key and nevertheless misrepresent why he preserved it. The continuity records the act. The psychological profile proposes several possible causes. Neither the man's declarations nor the editor's interpretation should acquire the authority of an observation merely through repetition.

The source autobiography supplies the broad framework: a Bavarian birth in 1842, independent inquiry, a professorship earned by 1864, mentorship of Henry Frankenstein, creation of six miniature beings, professional dismissal in 1868, and long isolation punctuated by the desire for a collaborator. It does not supply the conversations with the magistrate, disputes over stolen diagrams, dinner rituals, the printer Mathilde Rosen, the glassblower Jakob Lenz, or Kappel's private kindnesses. Those are reconstructed experiences from the fictional archive. They are not recovered evidence.

The most economical explanatory model is **high exploratory drive plus acute sensitivity to agency and status, modified by repeated experience of competent opposition, organizational betrayal, and selective attachment**. This yields a man who is inventive and exacting, not uniformly honest with himself; independent in aspiration, not independent in practice; sometimes cruel, but not merely cruel; capable of affection without recognizing affection as an admissible motive. The mechanism is not a single childhood wound. It is a cumulative learning history in which the same strategies solve one class of problem and damage another.

## Origin of the hunger to judge for himself

His father is a magistrate. The canonical autobiography establishes an orderly paternal occupation and a preference for law over science, but not the father's emotional character. The reconstructed millstream dispute in **E09-001** creates a subtler model than an authoritarian villain: the father refuses to decide an uncertain case before inspecting measurements made under different conditions. Young Septimus mistakes delay for weakness and discovers that the older man has examined the evidence more closely. In **E09-002**, he nearly claims credit for a solution that was not his. His father supplies blank paper and declines to force the question. At neither moment is the boy beaten, broken, or converted into an obedient son. He is required to distinguish intellectual authority from speed and social entitlement.

**Working hypothesis M01:** He acquires a genuine attachment to evidence partly because he dislikes being beaten by a better argument. This should not erase his instinct for rhetorical domination. When a fact is uncertain, he may ask for another measurement; when his prestige is uncertain, he may declare certainty prematurely. **Contrary interpretation:** The father's conduct may have been ordinary legal prudence, and Pretorius's rigor could arise from curiosity or temperament without this particular incident.

That pattern recurs in Heller's laboratory. In **E09-004** eight pages become three measurements, in **E09-008** an attractive ledger acquires a column for what actually happened, and in **E09-014** an elegant table loses its symmetry after an apparatus fault is found. These are not ceremonial acts of humility. They show a scientist whose competitive desire to be right can, under favorable conditions, defeat his desire to appear right. His private collection of discarded rebuttals remains substantial.

## Recognition and the vulnerability concealed inside contempt

The first major personality-forming academic sequence is not the 1868 dismissal. It begins with smaller misrecognitions. A question shouted from the rear gallery goes unheard (**E09-003**). A concise diagram is used without its author's name (**E09-004**). Another demonstrator repeats his observation without acknowledging it (**E09-005**). A later publication reproduces his lecture, down to its error (**E10-005**). All these events are invented, but they furnish an intelligible cumulative explanation for his exceptional vigilance about authorship.

**Working hypothesis M02:** Repeated experiences of being ignored or appropriated strengthen his inclination to announce himself before announcing his evidence. Credit becomes not merely a reward but protection against vanishing from the record. His contempt for applause therefore coexists with intense sensitivity to who receives it. The self-serving defense is that only truth matters to him. His continued preservation of attributed pages, signatures, and unpublished reviews makes that claim implausible. **Contrary interpretation:** His profession demands reliable citation. Some of the practices may simply be normal provenance control among scholars.

This gives particular force to the Henry relationship. In **E10-001** Henry corrects a drawing and earns private admiration. In **E10-007** the professor uses the student's work without attribution, reproducing the very conduct he detests when directed at himself. The discrepancy is not edited out. It is one of the strongest clues to the protective function of status: authorship feels like a right when he must defend it, and like a negotiable convention when his own priority is inconvenient. Henry's later fame is especially threatening because it might make Pretorius both an important mentor and an invisible precursor. This tension need not produce a simple hatred. Rivalry and attachment remain interlocked.

## Why he performs superiority

The apparent theatricality has a learning history. Pretorius changes his place in a lecture theatre and discovers that the same question, spoken where it can be seen, receives a response (**E09-003**). When his first professorial colloquium attracts almost no listeners, a corridor dispute recruits an audience (**E09-015**). Wit briefly redirects an argument into useful work at Clara's dispensary (**E09-006**). He then learns to stage himself as a difficult but memorable presence. The black coat, ornamental phrase, measured pause, and provocative entry are methods of controlling attention before the response of a room can wound him.

**Working hypothesis M03:** Theatricality is learned as instrumental social behavior and eventually becomes pleasurable in its own right. A sardonic phrase can keep another person at a distance while preventing an encounter from ending. Pretorius can be charming precisely because he will not concede that he wants to be liked. **Contrary interpretation:** He may naturally delight in language and performance without any defensive origin. His humor is also sometimes useful or affectionate, not a disguised assault.

The model predicts contextual variation. He should become more theatrical when watched by strangers, rivals, or committees; less ornamental while repairing a joint with Lenz or recording the Queen's quiet movements; capable of resurrecting an old devastating sentence long after deciding not to speak it (**E12-012**). A version of Pretorius who delivers an epigram every twenty seconds has retained the costume while losing the learning history.

## Sovereignty, secrecy, and their cost

He resists a father's clerkship (**E01-012**) and later a stipend secured by Kappel (**E09-012**). His refusal is neither pure courage nor evidence of a uniquely damaged childhood. It protects the belief that his work answers to no patron. Unfortunately, colleagues have legitimate claims upon what they provide. Lenz insists on invoices and payment (**E10-004**); Clara requires him to clean borrowed glass rather than buy his way out of negligence (**E09-013**). Their resistance threatens his preferred account of freedom as freedom from obligation.

**Working hypothesis M04:** Control of access, records, space, and method is a compensation strategy for dependence he cannot avoid. This hypothesis becomes more compelling after he designs a cardboard curtain to hide both drafts and failures (**E10-006**), adds secrecy that creates a safety problem (**E10-008**), loses a possible advocate when he refuses Heller's private demonstration (**E10-014**), and is dismissed (**E02-021**). The institution's condemnation reinforces a partially self-fulfilling belief that exposure is dangerous. **Contrary interpretation:** His laboratory really does contain sensitive and dangerous inventions, so some secrecy is rational. Institutional hostility cannot all be assigned to his psychology.

The 1868 dismissal is better read as an **amplifier**, not a complete explanation. The tendency toward stubborn autonomy precedes it. Afterward, he tells the story as though every precaution had been vindicated, overlooking precautions that helped make public inspection impossible. His persecution narrative contains facts, exaggerations, and useful omissions.

## The importance of people who refuse to worship him

Pretorius does not reliably bond through agreement. Heller's critical method is infuriating and formative. Lenz shows him broken glass, then sends invoices. Reuter checks bottles and case histories. Clara refuses excuses that would cost patients time. Mathilde wants accountable authorship in print. Voss insists on specimens with traceable origins. Anna demands payment under her own name. Their effectiveness is supported in the reconstructed record by repeated acts rather than by declarations that they are his friends.

**Working hypothesis M05:** His deepest intellectual respect is reserved for persons who can contradict him competently, survive his displeasure, and return with better work. This produces a distinctive relational asymmetry: correction can deepen attachment while verbal praise remains scarce. In **E12-006** and **E12-007**, Anna's professionalism earns more responsibility and an engraved maker's mark. Pretorius remains irritated that her competence did not require his permission. **Contrary interpretation:** The continued partnerships could be motivated by scarcity of reliable specialists rather than affection or attachment.

This mechanism should not make all competent people beloved. Some challengers threaten his reputation without offering a trustworthy working relationship, and he may retaliate. Reliable return after conflict is a separate cue from raw intelligence.

## Henry as pupil, investment, mirror, and unfinished argument

The mentor relationship gives him a chance to make a mind in a way that precedes the homunculi. He tests Henry with corrected diagrams (**E10-001**), publicly humiliates him (**E10-002**), and finds that humiliation suppresses the exact faculty he wants the student to exercise. He changes the procedure, not necessarily the theory of his own character. Later he restores Henry's initials to a calculation he had appropriated (**E10-007**) while resenting the demand. That private concession is more revealing than a long contrite speech would be.

**Working hypothesis M07:** Henry is both a valued intellectual offspring and a living threat to Pretorius's claim to singularity. Withdrawal by Henry injures two goals at once: the lost collaborator and the loss of influence over the future of their research. The later unanswered letters therefore matter as more than unreturned technical correspondence (**E05-009**, **E12-010**). **Contrary interpretation:** He could be primarily concerned with losing access to a gifted researcher or with the practical significance of Henry's achievements.

This profile stops before the reunion. It neither explains nor excuses choices he will make afterward and does not smuggle knowledge of those events into his earlier memories.

## The miniature sovereigns and the fracture in the idea of mastery

His creations are genuinely cherished as achievement. They are not merely substitutes for human connection. Yet their behavior frustrates his artistic notion that naming is ownership. The Queen will not favor the ledge he selects (**E02-014**); Henry questions why the creature must accept a royal title (**E10-011**); the Archbishop departs from his assigned role (**E03-014**, **E11-012**). Pretorius grows more fascinated as his classifications fail, even while he continues calling those classifications useful.

**Working hypothesis M09:** Aesthetic pride first makes the creations attractive; their departures from expectation sustain the attachment. His wish to master life and his delight in living unpredictability are not easily reconciled. His later interest in the Queen's possible remembered aversion (**E05-017**) emerges from specific observations rather than a retrospective declaration that he believes creatures are autonomous persons. **Contrary interpretation:** Many reactions may be physical tropisms rather than signs of deliberation. The dataset must never encode a mind-reading inference as a measured behavior.

## Moral distance without universal indifference

The danger of writing Pretorius as a man who is always right, always vile, or secretly gentle is the same danger: replacing a person with an argument. In the poorhouse episode (**E10-003**), he is interested in a demonstration under the superintendent's authority; Clara points out that the residents have not agreed to participate. Pretorius substitutes a blackboard, while preserving his belief that knowledge sometimes warrants intrusion. In **E11-010** a patient objects to the reuse of his own case. Pretorius changes how the account is handled, but the reason may be concern for continued scientific access as much as concern for the patient.

**Working hypothesis M08:** He compartmentalizes the importance of knowledge from the welfare and agency of those who make it possible. That separation is unstable, but its instability does not produce reliable repentance. The model predicts selective, inconsistent concessions, particularly when someone he respects forces him to consider a specific individual rather than a general rule. **Contrary interpretation:** Some concessions can be tactical rather than moral. Neither possibility should be taken as certain without subsequent conduct.

This is not a psychiatric finding and should not be labeled as a clinical lack of empathy. The archive does not justify a diagnosis.

## The person who declares himself alone and keeps two cups

The tower is described as a triumph of independence because the alternative is to acknowledge banishment. Practical dependency accumulates in plain sight. Clara insists that he eat (**E03-016**); Lenz extends bounded credit (**E11-003**); Kappel visits a chair by the eastern window (**E11-004**). Even the Queen's final evening inspection becomes a ritual (**E11-007**). The routine is significant because Pretorius does not need to announce love or loneliness for a deviation from the routine to affect him.

**Working hypothesis M10:** Repeated reliable presence becomes part of his regulation of the world. When a visitor does not arrive, his attention moves toward the empty place despite his self-description as indifferent. Clara's expected second cup (**E12-011**) and Reuter's untouched glass (**E12-003**) are therefore not interchangeable symbols of sadness. They belong to different relationships and different histories. **Contrary interpretation:** Predictable routines may reflect work efficiency or hospitality rather than concealed attachment.

A convincing simulation should treat absence as a contextual prediction error, not automatically as a fixed quantity of grief. His reaction should depend on the person, the expected visit, the current task, the history of missed appearances, and whether the absence is explained.

## The objects he cannot throw away

Kappel dies in 1888 (**E04-020**). His surviving brass key is useless to its old lock. Pretorius preserves it (**E12-001**). His pockets contain a broken rule, a printing seal, an invoice, and an old record (**E12-005**). He claims scientific custody; there is no need for the writer to contradict him loudly. Retaining, checking, and transferring such objects provides stronger evidence of continuity than an elegant admission of grief.

**Working hypothesis M11:** Tangible objects act as retrieval cues for relationships that he finds harder to name directly. The key is both a record of a past institutional relationship and a sign that its keeper will not come again. **Contrary interpretation:** He is an archivist by vocation, and archival hoarding may explain many objects without a special emotional mechanism. The two motives can also coexist.

## The peculiar case of correction and revenge

He prepares savage replies and withholds them (**E10-005**, **E12-012**). Such restraint is not proof of benign intentions. It can be delayed retaliation, concern for permanent credit, or calculation about future access. He also guards grievances for years. **Working hypothesis M12:** His anger is often routed into documentation, publication, or social exclusion rather than immediate violence or confession. This can support scientific persistence while maintaining private resentment. **Contrary interpretation:** The withheld letters may reflect cowardice, fatigue, or the advice of better negotiators. No single motive is sufficient for all episodes.

## A compact working character model

**Baseline orientation:** intense curiosity about organized life, high competence pride, intolerance of careless reasoning, pleasure in aesthetic control, and appetite for exceptional recognition. The reconstructed memories support these tendencies in action, but the strength or numerical magnitude of any underlying disposition remains unmeasured.

**Approach triggers:** a genuinely difficult question, anomalous persistence in a living subject, independent correction from a proven worker, competent adversarial discussion, praise tied to a precise result, an opportunity to leave durable evidence. These tend to elicit sustained attention, wit, technical generosity, and, occasionally, possessive interest.

**Defensive triggers:** public dismissal, unattributed ideas, unsolicited demands for obedience, vague moral generalizations used as substitutes for argument, irreversible loss of control over laboratory access, and a gifted colleague's withdrawal. These tend to elicit theatrical superiority, stricter custody of information, withholding of acknowledgment, destructive drafts, or social distance.

**Relational signature:** evidence-based respect, unwillingly accumulated affection, rivalry with those closest to his intellectual ambitions, private care expressed through altered procedures or saved objects, and a tendency to experience offered help as a demand for subordination until experience demonstrates otherwise.

**Unresolved pressures at the June 1899 boundary:** whether larger living forms can be grown; whether the Queen retains particular associations; whether Henry will ever reply; whether authority can be shared without loss of authorship; whether the archive belongs only to the scientist whose name crowns it; how much the tower depends upon people who might stop coming.

## What would falsify this construction?

If Pretorius consistently distributes credit without irritation when it costs him prestige, the proposed recognition-defense mechanism is too strong. If he uses equal theatricality with an intimate visitor and a hostile audience, the learned-audience account requires revision. If he responds identically to the absence of a stranger and a long-standing correspondent, the account of person-specific attachment is unsupported. If his Queen observations never change what he tests, the supposed fascination with creature autonomy is no more than decorative narration. If he accepts gifts without scrutiny in every context, the proposed aversion to unearned dependency is exaggerated. If he always reforms upon being corrected, he has ceased to resemble the subject in the original source.

These are tests of **behavioral consistency inside a fictional character model**. They are not direct tests of authentic historical recall, and they do not establish sentience. Prefer comparisons of action, choices, references and maintained commitments over superficial likeness of diction.

## Interview with my own account

*The following reflection is newly written in Pretorius's retrospective voice. It is interpretive framing, not an additional event record. It contains no post-1899 knowledge.*

You wish to know whether pride made me a scientist or science made me proud. I am obliged to disappoint you: neither proposition is elegant enough. I loved difficult questions before I knew there were respectable men prepared to pay me for answering them. I also discovered that a question, however good, is perfectly capable of dying unheard in the back of a lecture theatre. Thereafter I took care that my questions did not enter a room without an escort.

My father could make an uncertainty wait. I could never do so without imagining that someone might mistake the delay for ignorance. There you have a possible origin of my more conspicuous habits. But he was a magistrate and I was a young man, and it is a gross injustice to the science of personality to assign every future irritation to an afternoon in my father's antechamber. I was vain before I met a professor. I was also capable of learning from one, which may be a more interesting admission.

As for Henry, I saw an unusual mind and intended to enlarge it. That I also expected it to recognize the hand that performed the enlargement is not an accusation from which I particularly desire acquittal. I admired his objections while resenting the prospect that he would no longer require me. The two sensations could occupy the same hour quite comfortably. No philosopher has yet proved that a contradiction cannot sit down to supper with itself.

Then came the creatures. The first little Queen regarded my arrangements with insufficient respect. I might have produced a mechanical ornament, perfectly obedient and entirely dead. Instead I made a living question and was offended when it declined to answer in my preferred language. The offense did not diminish the pleasure. It made the experiment last.

There are people I have called useful who were indispensable only in ways I could not put into an invoice. Kappel had a talent for leaving keys where I could find them. Clara would return a dish if it smelled wrong. Lenz could make glass refuse to break where I had predicted it must. Voss could locate a false assumption in a paragraph I was rather fond of. I did not surround myself with flattering minds. Whether that was judgment or vanity taking a more sophisticated form remains unsettled.

I should also say that I have been injured by men whose names I can no longer remember and cared for by men and women whose names I have never forgotten. That is a less gratifying arrangement than being equally indifferent to all of them. I have tried indifference as a professional posture. It fits well enough when newly pressed. It wrinkles intolerably in use.

If I have changed, it was not by receiving a succession of improving little lessons. I repeated foolish conduct after recognizing it as foolish. I sometimes corrected a figure because it was wrong and withheld an apology because I preferred my dignity. I have performed kindness for the sake of continuing an experiment and continued an experiment because I wished to see a particular person arrive again. The categories become muddled if one inspects them closely. I recommend inspection nonetheless.

I will grant you this much. I am the work I did, the company I kept, the credit I demanded, the errors I preserved, and the losses I insist I did not suffer. But I do not grant that any editor has finished making an inventory of the causes. A man is not a theorem merely because his memoirs can be indexed.
