# V11 continuity and provenance: The Anatomist of the Soul

**Release:** v11, 430 first-person memories in 26 episodes. **Preserved baseline:** v10, 400 memories, including E26-001 to E26-015 from the parallel adult sensory arc. **New material:** E25-001 through E25-030, 5,051 words of recollection, covering 1865 to 1898. **Absolute boundary:** E05-021, June 1899, before any reunion with Henry Frankenstein.

## Documentary layers

The original scientific article, *On the Supposed Seat of the Soul, and the Persistence of Nervous Excitability*, is a fictional April 1867 English rendering of a putative German memoir; its experiments, counts, author, supposed journal placement and results are invented. Historical physiology informs the language but is not transformed into a false archival citation. The fictional *Le Figaro* feature attributed to Alcide Jolivet, dated August 28, 1867, accurately discusses that speculative article but is equally invented. The separate research dossier lists real historical publications and explains the deliberate Ingolstadt university divergence. These sources are documents in the fictional world, **not** independent historical evidence or observed model behaviour.

## Sequence and interpretation changes

**1865-1866:** E25-001 to E25-004 show conventional physiological questions, a criticized and corrected account of apparent memory, a patient's refusal to be treated merely as a specimen, and Clara and Lenz's temperature corrections. The scientific virtues are genuine, not retrospectively fabricated just to support a future downfall.

**February to July 1867:** E25-005 to E25-009 show the boundary between public conventional experiments and private miniature-organism work; a speculative conclusion survived sober journal editing. Printed offprints, a first requested autograph, conflicting correspondence and limited collegial invitations offer a positive taste of public recognition. Some peers criticize competently while continuing to value the paper.

**August 1867:** E25-010 and E25-011 place Pretorius in Paris as an invited participant in informal discussions, not an official congress demonstrator. E25-012 and E25-013 separate the reporter's personal attentiveness from pointed questioning about creating and owning an organism. The doctor's attraction is unconfirmed as reciprocal. E25-014 preserves both satisfaction and humiliation in the published critique; the journalist invents neither a created homunculus nor the quoted words.

**September 1867 to July 1868:** E25-015 to E25-020 follow a courteous refusal to retract, one withdrawn lecture, a delivery-crate rumour, ordinary friendship despite criticism, committee requests for documentation, and a dismissal already established in other event records. The French clipping may affect an institutional atmosphere; it is not the sole count in a formal accusation. Kappel and Heller are not villains. Pretorius's defensiveness is a contributing practice without being a complete explanation of the university's decisions.

**October 1868 to 1898:** E25-021 to E25-030 return intermittently to the unedited clipping. He composes and destroys a fantasy of winning the interview; keeps an unsent letter; accepts Mathilde's editing; decides against using his newspaper fame to correct a catalogue; hears a widow refusing a charlatan; receives Voss's informed criticism; discovers Kappel's inscribed copy; accepts Anna's welfare-based experimental objection; reconsiders evidence amid reports of X-rays; and finally permits his past to remain unresolved. The old pleasurable sensations are not erased by future embarrassment. He retains gratitude for Jolivet's listening without assigning the reporter an imaginary romance.

## Established continuity protections

No E25 event changes the Queen's first emergence in 1867 or the previously established order of the six miniature figures. No published physiological experiment describes the secret homunculus work. Theodor's departure in 1866 remains part of the adult relationship history, not a convenient invention to explain an attraction. Mathilde and Voss do not appear before their original introductions, Anna acts on her own judgement in 1891, and Kappel dies in 1888 as before. Pre-existing dismissal descriptions, Henry's unsent defence and all later tower-life memories retain their text. No action in this arc extends beyond the June 1899 stopping point.

The v10 400-event baseline is preserved exactly in event_id, title, approximate_date and memory_text. E26's fifteen post-university sensory records are retained and interleaved rather than overwritten. Chronological_order changes for ordering only. The v9 and v10 complete snapshots and reading editions remain accessible as immutable prior versions.

## Sensorimotor and association annotation

Thirty E25 sensory annotations yield 71 text-grounded percept descriptions across auditory, visual, tactile, olfactory, gustatory and bodily awareness. They do not assert simulated physiological measurements. Each memory has explicit past expectations, observed actions, decisions, consequences, changing relationships, alternative readings and links to existing IDs. These support hypothetical retrieval and behavioral tests, but do not independently establish that a connectome has learned those relations.

## Remaining uncertainty

The precise relation between newspaper opinion and the 1868 university inquiry remains disputed within the remembered story. Pretorius's conviction of betrayal may be self-protective; Jolivet's words may also have amplified existing suspicion. The reporter's private feelings are not available to the doctor. An authentic archive, observed affect, true historical papers and genuine neural memory effects must not be inferred from fictional source text. Future edit passes should preserve contrary recollections rather than imposing a single causal verdict.
