# The road from 287 to 1,000 memories

**Baseline:** The Lost Years v7, 287 event records in 18 episodes. **Gap:** 713 events. **Boundary:** June 1899, before the reunion.

The 713-event development allocation below is a planning budget, **not fixed quotas for formulaic scenes**:

| New event emphasis | Planning count | Longitudinal structure |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Laboratory trials, prototypes, failures, repair and improvement | 300 | Recurrent experiments over thirty years, with cross-episode consequences |
| Occult and boundary-science inquiries | 100 | Alchemical literature, mesmerists, spiritists, false leads, ambiguous results |
| Everyday sensory and bodily life | 95 | Food, smells, chronic strain, voice, clothing, weather, rooms, tools |
| Friends, pupils, rivals, queer relationships and obligations | 100 | Return visits, missed appointments, gifts, favors, conflicts, reconciliations |
| Dreams, nightmares and private symbolic associations | 55 | Recurring images that alter conduct, not supernatural proof |
| Travel, correspondence, ethical choices and eccentric routines | 63 | Locations and consequences that survive changes in company |
| **Total new memories** | **713** | |

Many records belong to more than one dimension. Experiment memories should *also* include social and sensory data when lived circumstances warrant it.

## Narrative design

Create sequences of 15 to 30 causally related events. At minimum one sequence should follow a prototype family through early promise, adjustment, apparent success, surprising failure, recalibration, consequence and long-term recall. Another should show why Pretorius refuses to destroy a flawed but living creation; another why he does destroy a beautiful experiment when it violates one of his principles. At least one should show an occult explanation temporarily outperforming his laboratory account without forcing supernatural certainty.

Keep a few scientific mysteries partially unresolved: the nature of the seed, why maturation and full-sized growth resist control, what the miniature subjects remember, whether attractiveness tracks internal stability, and exactly what happened at the faculty inquiry. Failure may be intensely unpleasant, but his persistence must emerge through routine: returning to a bench after dinner, ordering replacement glass, revising measurements, and letting weeks pass before admitting the result.

Use less dialogue in solitary research and more physical memory: damp wool, ink and smoke, honeyed spirits, chicken grease in the crypt, ringing ears, cracked lips, lamp soot, the temperature of old stone, the weight of a jar, the taste of metal after sleeplessness. Not every memory needs dramatic theatricality.

## Cohorts and gates

Work toward the intermediate milestones of 400, 600, 800 and 1,000 records. At each gate, audit original events preserved, sequence links, duplicate concepts, chronology, anachronisms, participants' lives, balanced outcomes, variety of bodily and emotional cues, moral contradictions and the strict historical stopping point.

Do not append previously published increments to the already complete v7 JSONL; the 27 new v7 records are already included in 287. Keep one clearly named **current integrated dataset**; version past releases as immutable snapshots. After each batch, regenerate the chronological manuscript and structured dataset together.

## Research anchors, source material not implanted events

- American Society of Cinematographers, *The Bride of Frankenstein: A Gothic Masterpiece*, https://theasc.com/article/bride-of-frankenstein/ ; filming of glass-jar miniature people and effects.
- Turner Classic Movies, *Bride of Frankenstein*, https://www.tcm.com/articles/35375/bride-of-frankenstein-1935 ; story and crypt episode.
- Janice R. Welsch and Syndy M. Conger (1984), *The Comic and the Grotesque in James Whale's Frankenstein Films*, https://www.enotes.com/topics/james-whale/criticism/criticism/janice-r-welsch-and-syndy-m-conger-essay-date-1984 ; Pretorius's calculated humor and persistence.
- Andreas Sommer (2013), *Normalizing the Supernormal*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3580885/ ; German psychical research from 1886, *Sphinx* and spiritist debates.
- Burkhart Brückner (2015), *Animal Magnetism, Psychiatry and Subjective Experience*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4847384/ ; historical nineteenth-century German magnetism.
- *Sphinx* journal archive, https://iapsop.com/archive/materials/sphinx_leipzig/ ; primary occult-literary research leads.

No article establishes the new episodes as historical fact. These materials support chronology, social context, and the character design rather than autobiography by themselves.
